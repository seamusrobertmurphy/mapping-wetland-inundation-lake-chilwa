## Retrieved but not cited, and why

Reports that South Africa's National Wetland Map 5 "represented 87% more inland, surface aquatic ecosystems than the Global Surface Water (GSW) product" and that "the GSW products do not include palustrine and arid systems at all". ### Held in reserve, quantitatively strong but blocked by the public-product rule

- **Tootchi, Jost and Ducharne (2019)**, *Earth System Science Data* 11, 189-220, doi:10.5194/essd-11-189-2019. The optical global layer "misses most understory inundations," and its unique contribution to their regularly-flooded-wetland map is 0.4 per cent of land surface against 9.7 per cent for the merged product. Full text verified. Cannot be used without naming the product.
- **Zhang et al. (2021)**, *Earth System Science Data* 13, 2001-2023, doi:10.5194/essd-13-2001-2021 (WAD2M). Coarse microwave products underestimate because of closed canopies; their correction adds 3.6 Mkm² globally. Full text verified. Usable without naming an optical product if wanted.
- **Fleischmann et al. (2022)**, *Remote Sensing of Environment* 278, 113099, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2022.113099. Agreement across 18 Amazon inundation datasets is highest on open-water floodplains, intermediate on vegetated floodplains, and worst on shallow interfluvial wetlands; even the best radar dataset underestimates by about 10 per cent. Abstract only, from a repository deposit.
- **Prigent et al. (2007)**, *Journal of Geophysical Research* 112, D12107, doi:10.1029/2006JD007847. Detection floor of about 10 per cent fractional inundation per 0.25 degree cell, "the higher the vegetation density the lower the sensitivity." Full text verified.

### Correction to an earlier note

The "11 to 13 per cent of National Wetland Map 5" figure could not be traced to any retrievable source and must not be used. Van Deventer (2021) supports 87 per cent under-reporting and 12 per cent of 157,728 wetland and river polygons carrying any inundation signature, both verified in the published text.

### Further material held in reserve under the public-product rule

- **Prigent, Jimenez and Bousquet (2020)**, *Journal of Geophysical Research: Atmospheres* 125(3), e2019JD030711, doi:10.1029/2019JD030711. States of the standard 30 m optical product that "these estimates cover open water bodies but exclude wetlands that are vegetated," and extends the underestimation finding beyond forest to "dense low vegetation as in the Niger Delta or in the Ganges region, during the wet season." That extension to herbaceous cover is the most apposite sentence in the whole corpus for a *Typha* swamp, and it is unusable while the rule stands. Full text verified via the HAL deposit.
- **Aires et al. (2018)**, *Remote Sensing of Environment* 216, 427-441, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2018.06.015. Isolates the vegetation term directly: across an Amazon tree-cover gradient, optically detected transitory inundation falls linearly from 50 per cent to zero as tree cover rises, while the microwave-merged product retains 13 per cent in the densest bin. Maximum extents 5.77 against 13.67 million km². Full text verified. This is the cleanest published separation of the canopy effect from the cloud effect.
- **Zhang et al. (2021)** WAD2M, already listed above: optical products "observe the open-water dynamics but have limited capacity to detect surface water beneath vegetation canopy." Global open-water maximum 4.5 Mkm² against vegetated-wetland maximum 13.0 Mkm², which are different quantities by definition rather than competing estimates of one.
- **Prigent et al. (2016)**, *Surveys in Geophysics* 37(2), 339-355, doi:10.1007/s10712-015-9339-x, reporting Nakaegawa (2012): visible and near-infrared estimates of total wetland surface run "of the order of 10 times lower" than the inventory, with "only a 5 % chance that a wetland pixel in GLWD corresponds to one in the other five datasets." Report as cited-in; the primary source was not retrieved.

### Counterpoints to carry, so the argument is not overstated

- Coarse passive microwave is not a solved alternative. Prigent et al. (2007) undershoot L-band radar over an Amazon window by about 30 per cent at high water, and Bernard et al. (2024) show one microwave product returning almost flat seasonal cycles over the Amazon and Congo because the vegetation term is not correctly subtracted.
- Uday et al. (2025), *PLOS ONE* 20(1), e0314033, measure optical water omission errors of 50.7 and 64.3 per cent in cloud-free conditions, but attribute them chiefly to spatial resolution and small water bodies rather than to canopy. Do not cite as a canopy effect.
- Pekel et al. (2016) report missing less than 5 per cent of water in their own validation, which was against visible-band reference imagery and therefore cannot detect sub-canopy omission. A reader will assume that figure settles the question unless the circularity is stated.
